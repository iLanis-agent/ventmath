Vent math

An attic that bakes in summer and sweats in winter usually has vents bought by eye. Vent math turns the attic floor and a labeled ratio into the net free area to aim for, turns the need and a labeled intake share into the soffit-versus-ridge split, and turns each side's need and a labeled vent rating into a vent count. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Area plan - the attic floor at the labeled 1 in 150 rule becomes the net free area to aim for.
  • Split plan - the need at a labeled intake share becomes soffit and ridge areas.
  • Vent plan - the need against a labeled vent rating becomes the count of vents.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: one unit of net free area for each 150 units of attic floor, a 50% intake share, vents rated at 150 sq cm each - all illustrative defaults you can change. Real roofs have cathedral ceilings, vapor questions and local codes. This is illustrative arithmetic, not a ventilation design: local codes and the vent maker's net-free-area rating govern, and a roofer or building scientist sizes the real system.
